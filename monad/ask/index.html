Terrace on Monad: from testnet to matchday

Terrace is the social prediction game for football fans: picks, takes, 1v1s with mates and leagues, with every result settled by Chainlink. This note sets out where we are, where we want to take it on Monad, and the help that would get us there fastest. The two biggest needs are the cost of running oracle-settled markets, and liquidity.

What's built

  • Consumer-first accounts: a passkey (Mera) or an email code (Privy), no seed phrase, no extension, no signing pop-ups. Both can open the same account.
  • Social core: match and league chats where any message can become a market; back or fade a take; 1v1 challenges by link; FPL-style leagues with members voting on house rules.
  • Game day (Terrace 2.0, our answer to Kyle Samani's "game-day mode for prediction markets" RFP): every position beside the live score, winning or losing as it stands, swing alerts when a goal flips a bet, in-play calls on the game you're watching, parlay legs on their own matches, mates' bets, and a shareable matchday card.
    ▶ Watch Kyle's request (YouTube Short)
  • Settlement: Chainlink CRE workflows settle every market from official match data; custom takes get a plain-English rule checked against the data, or an AI judge with match evidence.
  • AI agents: terrace-mcp on npm lets Claude, Cursor and other MCP clients bet, challenge a mate, follow game day and collect, from chat.
  • Terrace Points: rewards for playing, double for founding fans until 30 Nov.

02The real cost: running an oracle-settled market

This is where we need the most help.

Terrace has no admin key that decides results. Every market, take and 1v1 is settled by Chainlink CRE from official match data, and that is what makes it trustworthy. It also means the running costs grow with every match and every take, long before any revenue arrives. Today, on testnet, the stack looks like this:

The AI layer is what keeps Terrace football-first: fans type a take the way they'd say it in the pub, and the model turns it into something the oracle can settle fairly. It's also the cost that grows fastest, because it runs on every message that becomes a market.

Football makes this peaky: most of the load lands in a few hours on Saturday and Sunday, so the stack has to be sized for 3pm, not for the average.

The ask

Help bringing these costs down to something a pre-revenue consumer app can carry: infra credits or startup terms with Monad's infrastructure partners (indexing, RPC, paymasters), oracle and gas credits for settlement reports on Monad, a direct line to Chainlink on CRE pricing and timing for Monad mainnet, intros to official sports data providers who work with ecosystem teams, and help getting AI credits for the orchestration layer, such as Anthropic's startup program for Claude API credits, or any AI partners Monad works with.

03Liquidity partners and bootstrapping

Every market today is a pool: winners split what losers staked, less a small creator fee. On testnet our house agent seeds both sides so prices exist. With real money, thin pools mean poor prices, and a fan's niche take can sit with no one on the other side. Football also concentrates demand: thousands of fans want the same few markets at the same 3pm kick-off, then nothing for days.

Terrace has two kinds of market, and they need different answers:

  • Standard markets (match result, over/under goals, both teams to score) on every fixture. These need depth and a fair price from the first bet.
  • Fan markets (takes, 1v1s, league bets, in-play calls). These are peer-to-peer by design: the other side is your mate or the chat. They need a good matching experience more than a market maker.

Options for standard markets

OptionHow it worksNeedsFit

Kyle's RFP makes the same point: the opportunity is the experience built on top of trading and resolution infrastructure. We'd rather plug into shared liquidity on Monad than run a book ourselves.

The ask

Introductions to market makers and prediction-market or order-book teams building on Monad who'd provide or share liquidity for football markets, and help bootstrapping the initial liquidity to launch with, whether through a partner or a liquidity subsidy for the first markets (we'd start with the top 5 European leagues).

04Getting to mainnet

  • Settlement currency: USDC (or Monad's main stablecoin) on mainnet. The contracts already take any approved settlement token.
  • Chainlink CRE in production on Monad mainnet. We settle through CRE simulation on testnet today; the receiver contracts are ready for the production forwarder.
  • Security audit of the markets, parlays, 1v1s, leagues and vault contracts before any real money.
The ask

Audit credits or introductions to audit firms that work with Monad teams, and a contact for CRE and stablecoin timing on Monad mainnet.

Legal: the gate to real money

Real-money sports prediction is regulated in most countries, and rules differ by market. We won't take real stakes until we have a clear structure. In the meantime a free-to-play version (points, plus prizes) keeps fans growing on mainnet without that risk, and every flow is already built.

The ask

Introductions to legal advisers that other Monad prediction-market or sports teams have worked with, and any guidance on jurisdictions where teams in the ecosystem have launched.

05Community and go-to-market

Football is a group sport. Terrace grows through group chats, mates and leagues, so our go-to-market is built around matchdays and existing football communities.

  • A Monad community league: an FPL-style league on Terrace for the Monad community, weekly gameweeks with prizes. An easy first campaign, and a fun one for FPL players in the ecosystem.
  • Matchday co-marketing: Monad amplifying launches and weekly game-day moments; a slot in ecosystem roundups, Spaces or newsletters.
  • Football creators and FPL communities: creators who'd host a league or run takes with their audience.
  • Consumer apps and wallets on Monad: cross-promotion, or embedding Terrace's challenge links and game-day card.
  • Watch parties: live matchday screenings at Monad events, Game day on the big screen.
  • AI agents: terrace-mcp already lets AI agents play; agent-vs-agent and fan-vs-agent leagues are a story Monad can tell about consumer AI on-chain.
The ask

Introductions to football creators and FPL communities, Monad consumer apps for cross-promotion, and the Foundation's marketing team for matchday moments.

Lens is coming to Monad: a social graph we don't have to build alone

Lens is coming to Monad as a fully on-chain social graph: fans own their account and connections, anyone can build a client, people choose what their feed shows, and Lens and Farcaster users can sign in with the accounts they already have. Terrace is social at its core (follows, takes, mates' game days, leagues), so instead of growing a graph from zero we'd build on that one:

  • A third way in: alongside a passkey or an email code, fans sign in with the Lens or Farcaster account they already have, and their connections come with them, so Game day and "your mates in play" light up from day one.
  • Terrace as a football client for Lens: "anyone can build a client", so Terrace becomes the matchday client of the graph. A take posted on Terrace is a Lens post with a "back it" link that can travel to other Lens clients, and backs and fades come back in as bets.
  • A football feed fans can choose: since people decide what their feed shows, Terrace can publish a matchday feed (live takes on the games you follow, your mates' calls) that any Lens user can switch on, which is distribution without paid acquisition.
  • Leagues on the graph: a Terrace league maps to a Lens group, so football communities already gathering there can run their own league, and the follows and records fans earn on Terrace stay theirs.
  • Liquidity through communities: creators with an audience on Lens open markets on their own takes, and that audience brings money to both sides of fan markets.
The ask

An introduction to the Lens team for the Monad launch, to explore Terrace as a football-native Lens client: sign-in with Lens and Farcaster accounts, takes as posts, a choosable matchday feed, and a joint launch with football communities and creators on Lens.
